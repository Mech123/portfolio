Summary (based on evaluation table)

Based on the combined evaluation table, the best overall choice is FINAL_SWITCH_RESIDUAL because it has the lowest overall errors (Overall_MAE 520.0, Overall_RMSE 672.2) with the highest overall fit (Overall_R² 0.9949), narrowly outperforming plain LSTM (Overall_MAE 521.3, RMSE 674.7). If the main priority is peak-demand accuracy, ENS_RESIDUAL_LSTM is the peak specialist (Peak_MAE 548.5, Peak_RMSE 707.8, Peak_R² 0.535), but it sacrifices overall accuracy compared to LSTM/switching. SARIMA and HYBRID are not good here because they show extremely large errors and strongly negative R² (they fail to track magnitude/peaks properly), and when these weak models are included, simple ENS_MEAN/ENS_MEDIAN get dragged down and become much worse than LSTM. Ridge stacking improves over naive averaging, but it still cannot fully handle the regime-dependent behavior (normal vs peak hours) as effectively as the switching/residual strategy, while ENS_NNLS collapses to LSTM-level performance, indicating the weight optimizer effectively trusts LSTM most.
